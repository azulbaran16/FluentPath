## The D-06 originality judgement — my honest read

The grep is the weaker half and the plan is right to say so. I read the authored prose rather
than scanning it: **the whole Speaking self-evaluation rubric** (all 16 items with their
explanations), **all four Reading parts in full** (passages, 23 stems, 15 blanks and all 38
explanations), the **Listening news item** in full and the **three-speaker discussion** in
full, and **all eight Speaking prompts** with their strategy tips.

**My judgement: this is original work, and I am not close to the line on it.** The reasons,
so you can check my reasoning rather than take my word:

- **It has an author's voice, sustained across 2,600 lines.** A lifted-and-reworded bank reads
  flat and interchangeable. This does not. Explanations argue: *"the grammar never tells you,
  the surrounding facts do"*, *"a pause between two ideas reads as composure; a pause
  mid-clause reads as being stuck"*, *"coherence is heard, not planned"*. That is somebody
  thinking, not somebody paraphrasing.
- **The content is load-bearing in ways copied text cannot be.** The correspondence blanks are
  answerable only by holding a fact from the first email while reading the second — the price,
  the head count, who dropped out. The viewpoints part is built on four positions whose
  agreements deliberately cut across each other. The discussion has a speaker who withdraws an
  argument mid-meeting when a number contradicts it. That architecture was designed here.
- **Every proper noun is invented** — Kettle Bend, Ellisburn, Bellwood, Harlow Bay, Nadia
  Farouk, Colin Beausoleil, Yusra Belkacem, the Ransome family. The real place names that do
  appear (Winnipeg, Meech Lake, Wakefield) are geography, used as setting.
- **The rubric in particular reads as ours.** The official level descriptors are third-person
  analytic bands. These are second-person questions answerable while listening back to a
  sixty-second recording — *"is there one word you reached for over and over — 'good',
  'thing', 'nice', 'a lot'?"*. That is a different rhetorical object, not a reworded one.

### The one thing I want to name, because you asked me to name anything I am unsure about

**The rubric's four dimension names are the official CELPIP Speaking sub-score names.** "Task
Fulfilment", "Content & Coherence", "Vocabulary" and "Listenability" are the exam's own scoring
axes, taken over as labels.

My assessment is that this is **fine, and in the same category as everything else this phase
borrowed**: part names, item counts and timings. Short functional labels for scoring axes are
facts about how the test is marked, not protectable expression — and the whole app is built on
that class of fact. It would also be *worse* for the learner to invent different names, since
recognising the axes is part of what she is rehearsing. `rubric-speaking.ts` says all of this
in its own header, unprompted, which is the right instinct.

But it is the single place where something from the source material is reproduced verbatim
rather than rewritten, so it belongs in this record rather than in a footnote. **If you want
zero verbatim borrowing, these four strings are the only thing to change**, and changing them
costs nothing technically — they are display labels, and the ids (`speaking-task-fulfilment`
etc.) are what persist in attempt history.

Everything else I read: original.

### [Observation, not fixed] `planVoices` is all-or-nothing, and the discussion part is where it bites

Your concern is precise and the code confirms it. In `src/lib/celpip-speech.ts`:

```ts
const enoughVoices = distinct.length > 0 && pool.length >= distinct.length;
```

The discussion part has **three** distinct speakers. If the device offers only two English
voices, `enoughVoices` is false and **all three** speakers fall back to the browser default —
it does not use the two voices it has plus one pitch shift. They are then distinguished by
`PITCH_LADDER` alone: 1, 0.85, 1.15. **Six of that part's eight questions are attribution
questions.**

The authored insurance is real: plan 11 has all three speakers **named aloud within the first
seven turns**, each immediately before or after they first speak, so attribution stays
recoverable from content even if the voices are not recoverable by ear. I verified that in the
script.

I did not change this. Whether ±15% pitch on one voice is followable is exactly the question
only your ear can answer, and it is item 3 on the checklist below. If it fails, the smallest
honest fix is a partial assignment (use however many real voices exist, pitch-shift the
remainder) rather than the current all-or-nothing fallback.
