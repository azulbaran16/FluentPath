Three staff at a swimming pool arguing about an early-morning lane swim that averages nine
swimmers: a supervisor who wants to cut it to three mornings, an instructor who fights the
cut, and a colleague who takes half of the instructor's case and builds a different proposal
on it.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Segments | 1 |
| Script | **378 words** across **26 turns** (target 300-380) |
| Speakers | **3** — Yusra Belkacem, Marek Dolinski, Bethan Cray |
| **Longest turn** | **20 words** (authoring target 25, harness ceiling 35) |
| Questions | **8** — the exam's confirmed count for this shape |
| **Attribution items** | **6 of 8** (plan's minimum: 4) |

**The cross-talk is real, not three monologues taking turns.** Marek claims the low figure is
seasonal; Yusra answers that October, March and last May all averaged nine; he repeats the
month back as a question — "last May as well?" — and withdraws the argument. Bethan then tells
him not to withdraw *all* of it, restates his surviving point in her own words ("take Tuesday
away and you don't lose Tuesday, you lose the habit"), and proposes something neither of the
other two had put forward. **The proposal that carries is not the one the meeting opened
with**, which is what makes q6 a different question from q1.

**Six items require attribution rather than recall:**

- **q1** — who proposes the cut. The board is named twice and both times by Yusra herself,
  never as the source of anything; Bethan really does handle the boiler timer, but at the end
  and as a task.
- **q2** — whose evidence the season-ticket figure is. Every wrong option is a real number
  from the same conversation belonging to somebody else: eleven is Bethan's, the three months
  that all averaged nine are Yusra's — and she uses them *against* him.
- **q3** — who changes position, and what changes it.
- **q4** — which half of Marek's case Bethan keeps. The hardest item in the set: **the words
  arrive in the wrong voice**, because she is quoting him.
- **q6** — whose proposal is adopted, with "take it to the board" and "let the board decide"
  as separable options, distinguished in one sentence of the audio.
- **q7** — which of three tasks belongs to which of three people, all handed out in the last
  half-minute.

**q5 and q8 are deliberately straight recall** (the two attendance figures, and the March
fallback). A part where all eight items test one skill is a drill, not a rehearsal.

### The speaker names are spoken aloud, and that was not a stylistic choice

**The player shows no speaker label while the audio is playing.** `ListeningPlayer.tsx` renders
`turn.speaker` in exactly two places — the post-answer transcript and the audio-failure read
mode — and under D-04 neither is reachable before she has answered. The plan's `<human-check>`
says the three speakers are told apart "by label plus pitch offset where [the browser does not
have three voices]"; **that live label does not exist.**

This is a correctness problem, not a cosmetic one: six of eight options sets name a person, and
a name she has never heard attached to a voice makes those six unanswerable rather than hard.

So the script binds each voice to a name itself. All three are named aloud **within the first
seven turns**, each immediately before or after their first line — Yusra addresses Bethan, who
then speaks; Marek answers Yusra by name and Yusra names him back. That is how a meeting
introduces itself when nobody can see the room, and it is what the exam's own video does with
faces. The reasoning is written into the part's header comment rather than left here, so the
next author of a multi-speaker part meets it where they would otherwise repeat the mistake.

**What was deliberately not done:** a live "now speaking" label. It would need per-utterance
callbacks in `celpip-speech.ts`, which fires `onStarted` on the first utterance only and
`onCompleted` on the last — and under D-05 that last callback is the sole signal that reveals
the questions. Threading a per-turn callback through the one module whose failure mode is a
learner stranded on an empty screen is an architectural change (Rule 4), not a fix, and it
belongs to a plan that owns the component. Recorded as ledger id 15 rather than attempted.

shape, and an exact count would fail that and end up relaxed rather than respected. It also
retroactively covers plans 06's two-speaker parts.

into the content commit.

### [Judgement call] Six attribution items, not four

The plan asked for at least four of eight. Six were written, and two were deliberately left as
straight recall. Four out of eight leaves the part half recall, which is a news item with three
voices; eight out of eight is a drill on one skill. The mix is the same judgement plans 06 and
07 made and is stated here so the next reader sees the counter-pressure was in view.

### [Judgement call] The speakers name each other aloud

Described in full above. Not in the plan, which assumed a live on-screen label that the player
does not render. Authoring around it was the only route that did not require changing the speech
driver.

### [Judgement call] The time-limit comment drops "provisional" without gaining a count

The plan says to update `timeLimitMinutes` "for the complete six-part set from the confirmed
per-part allowances". The value is **derived** and the plan's own non-negotiables forbid setting
it, so there was nothing to update — it moved 44 → 55 on its own. What could be updated was the
comment, and the provisional framing is gone as asked. Writing "the set now carries all six" in
its place would have re-created exactly the stale-number trap plans 06 and 07 removed from this
same comment, so it gained a **rule** instead: 47-55 minutes is the exam's window for six parts,
and a full set landing far outside it means the derivation is wrong.

### [Scope] Nothing else

No package installed. No component touched. No file owned by the parallel plan staged, read for
edit, or restaged.
