## What shipped

### Task 1 — the information part (commit `06761d2`)

`ls1-information`, "What happens to a donated bicycle". A workshop coordinator briefing a new
volunteer.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Segments | 1 |
| Script | **379 words** across **20 turns**, one speaker |
| **Longest turn** | **22 words** (authoring target 25, harness ceiling 35) |
| Questions | **6** — the exam's confirmed count for this shape |

The talk has four ordered stages (intake, assessment, repair, safety check), a three-way
classification assigned at exactly one of them (green / amber / red), an order of operations
*inside* the longest stage (outward from the wheels, and she gives the reason), and a rule about
where a failure sends the work back to (bench three, not bench two, code unchanged).

**Three of the six items can only be answered by someone who tracked the order**, one more than
the plan asked for:

- **q1** — the four benches in sequence. All four names appear in every option; **only the order
  differs**, so recognising the words is worth nothing.
- **q4** — the order of the repairs themselves, where the wrong option is the reversal.
- **q5** — where a failed safety check sends the bicycle. Going back to intake is a real rule in
  this talk, but it belongs to an untagged bicycle, not to a failed check.

The distractors follow the house rule from plans 05 and 06: every one is a fact the talk really
does state, filed at the wrong bench. q2's wrong options are made *entirely* of true statements
— the colour code (bench two, one stage later) and the Saturday sale (decided at the very end by
a coordinator) — which is why a note recording *when* something was said beats a note recording
only what.

**Not a reading passage in disguise.** The script signposts its own structure out loud ("bench
two is assessment"), addresses the listener, and repeats the bench numbers, because a listener
cannot scan back and a reader can. The header comment on the part states this and records the
rejection of script reuse across skills, so it is on the record rather than rediscovered under
time pressure.

### Task 2 — the viewpoints part (commit `b88cfcd`)

`ls1-viewpoints`, "The street that stopped taking cars". A columnist at a public meeting on
whether to make an eighteen-month road closure permanent.

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Segments | 1 |
| Script | **473 words** across **24 turns** — the longest in the set |
| **Longest turn** | **22 words** |
| Questions | **5** — the fewest items on the longest audio, which is the exam's own ratio |

That ratio is the difficulty and it is stated in the part's header comment: there is no rhythm of
question after question to keep attention up, and a listener who drifts for twenty seconds has no
way of knowing whether the thing she missed was one of the five.

**Four of the five turn on fact against opinion or on attribution**, one more than the plan's
minimum of three:

- **q2** — which claim was *measured*. Every wrong option is real content: emissions she refuses
  to endorse, the street being pleasant to stand in (her own view), spending (the thing nobody
  counted). Hearing a number is not the same as hearing that it was counted here.
- **q3** — a group named as holding a position and then shown to be **19 / 17 / 4 split**, against
  a chair who has argued for reopening at three public meetings.
- **q4** — a claim she neither accepts nor rejects but **reclassifies**, from a finding to a
  belief. Rejecting it outright is the tempting misreading, because she warns you about the claim
  before she states it — and a warning is not a denial.
- **q5** — her own position, which is **conditional**: keep the street closed, but not in March
  and not before the displaced parking is dealt with. The trap option drops the condition, and it
  is a good one, because she does say "keep Ferren Street closed" and it takes the next sentence
  to learn she would vote against it in March anyway.

**q1 is deliberately straight recall** (the March vote). A part where all five items test the
same skill is a drill, not a rehearsal, and the exam's own mix is not homogeneous.

The speaker states the whole fact/opinion distinction out loud once, near the start — "some of
what I am about to tell you was measured, some of it is what people believe" — which is what a
spoken talk has to do. That signposting is also the trap: she says it once, and everything after
depends on having believed her.

### The time-limit comment

The plan asked to "update the set's `timeLimitMinutes` to reflect five parts". There was nothing
to update — the value is derived, which is the property plan 05 built and plan 06 confirmed. What
the plan really asked for was underneath that: **comment what it is derived from**. So the
comment now names both terms explicitly — every turn of every segment totalled and divided by the
app's *actual* rate (150 wpm nominal at the 0.95 that `celpip-speech.ts` sets, so about 142 words
a minute), plus one minute per question — and says why a hand-typed figure would be a defect no
assertion can find: a limit shorter than the audio and the audio itself would both look
reasonable.

Plan 06's judgement is kept: **no count of what exists is written into the prose**. The comment
says the figure moves on its own whenever a part is appended or removed, "including the exam part
shapes this set does not carry yet". That phrasing stays true after plan 11 lands; naming the
discussion part there would have left plan 11 a comment to edit, which is the trap plan 06
removed.

## Deviations from Plan

### [Judgement call] Three order-dependent items, and four fact-versus-opinion items

The plan asked for "at least two" questions in the information part answerable only by having
tracked the order, and "at least three" of five in viewpoints hinging on fact against opinion.
Three and four were written. In both cases the extra item cost nothing and is the difference
between the part having its shape and merely wearing its label — the same judgement plan 06 made
about the conversation's implication items.

The counter-pressure was kept in view: viewpoints q1 is deliberately plain recall, so the part is
a rehearsal rather than a drill on one skill.

### [Judgement call] The viewpoints script is 473 words against a ~450 target

Reported rather than rounded. The first draft was 489; four turns were tightened and one
rhetorical line cut, which took it to 473. Cutting the remaining 23 words meant removing either
the shopkeepers' 19/17/4 split or the café owners' interest disclosure, and both are load-bearing
for the part's shape. The information part is inside its band at 379 (target 300–380, first draft
407). "Roughly 450" absorbs 5%; a worse script would not have.

### [Judgement call] The time-limit comment names its derivation but still writes no count

Described in full above. The plan says to "note that it will move once the discussion part
lands"; the comment instead says it moves whenever any part is appended or removed, including
shapes not yet carried. Naming the discussion part would have been true today and stale the day
plan 11 lands — the precise trap plan 06 removed from this same comment.

### [Scope] Nothing outside the content module

Plan 06 had to add an ordering gate outside its declared file. This plan needed no such
exception: `files_modified` named `src/lib/celpip/listening-set-1.ts` and that is the only file
either commit touches. The gate plan 06 built is what made appending these two parts safe, and
two of this plan's mutations exercise it.
