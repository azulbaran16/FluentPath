## What shipped

### Task 1 — the correspondence part (commit `0b2acb0`)

`src/lib/celpip/reading-set-1.ts`, and `READING_SETS` spread from it exactly as the listening
bank composes.

**`rs1-correspondence` — 11 minutes, eleven items in the exam's confirmed six-then-five
order.** A 270-word original email (Nadia to Colin: a cabin weekend moved to a different lake,
two people dropped out, the per-head price gone from sixty-five dollars to eighty, a deposit
due on the twelfth, and a request to drive) followed by six comprehension questions on it, and
then Colin's reply carrying five drop-down blanks. The six-then-five order is not
interchangeable and the file says so: she should meet the blanks in the position she will meet
them on the day.

**The blanks are the whole reason this part shipped first, and authoring them is the expensive
part.** Every distractor is grammatically fine and only contextually wrong, and each one is
ruled out by a *named* fact:

| Blank | What the tempting wrong option contradicts |
|---|---|
| b1 | She has *not* checked with the group — that is why the email exists; the old cabin is off the rental list; the deposit is not yet paid |
| b2 | Last year was sixty-five dollars, so eighty is neither "the same" nor "the least we have ever paid" |
| b3 | The deposit is due on the twelfth, so "leave it until we are up there" is too late; and the cabin has **no internet**, so nothing can be sent from it |
| b4 | **Marcus has pulled out**, so "I will come up with Marcus" is disqualified by a fact three paragraphs earlier |
| b5 | Eight was the head count *before* the two drop-outs; six is what the whole price calculation rests on |

That last column is the difference between a blank that trains her and a spot-the-typo
exercise. Every one of the twenty items carries an original explanation, and on the blanks the
explanation names what ruled the alternative out rather than merely asserting the key.

### Task 2 — the information part (commit `497e308`)

**`rs1-information` — 9 minutes, nine questions, no blanks.** A 569-word original text on an
invented heritage site (the Kettle Bend Woollen Mill), presented in **five labelled sections**
as the real thing is, so a question can ask which part of the text carries something.

**Two questions offer a "the passage does not say" option and it is the KEY on one of them.**
That is deliberate and it is the point of the part: an option that is never the answer teaches
the learner to discount the very thing the information part tests her on, and she would then
meet it on exam day having trained herself the wrong way. Where it is the key (q3, why the
family chose the site), the explanation names what would have had to appear for it to be
wrong — "a sentence of the form *they built here because…*, and no sentence in the five
sections does that" — and it warns about the trap that makes it hard, which is that the
passage *does* explain how the river drove the machinery, just never why the site was chosen.
Where it is not the key (q5, q7), the explanation names the section that states the fact.

The rest of the nine mix stated detail, cross-section inference (a visitor who cannot manage
stairs, assembled from three sentences of section B), arithmetic inference (1986 plus five
years, with every wrong option a real date from the text), and one whole-text judgement.

**No listening script was adapted here**, and that was a live temptation with six finished
listening parts in the next file. A spoken talk repeats itself, signposts itself and carries a
speaker's voice; an information text does none of those. Rejected on the record in a comment
on the part, per D-07 and RESEARCH's own note.

**The set's total stays derived.** `readingSetMinutes` sums the parts: 20 minutes today, the
exam's 39 the moment plan 10 lands, with no edit in this file.

## The RSC payload leak, re-measured — with two controls, because a zero proves nothing alone

Plan 05 measured a resolved set being inlined into the served HTML at 41 of 42 authored
strings. A reading set is worse: it is the answer key **and** 839 words of passage. The route
passes `set.id` and the runner resolves it, so this should be zero — and "should be zero" is
exactly the claim that needs proving twice.

**Passive control** — three strings that are in the document *by design* (the set title and
the two part-kind labels, which the route puts into its JSON-LD envelope on purpose). If those
were absent, the probe would be looking at the wrong page or a failed fetch, and any zero
would be worthless.

**Active control** — the defect deliberately re-introduced: `ReadingRunner` given an optional
`set` prop, the route changed to pass the resolved set, rebuilt, re-measured, then reverted
with `git checkout --` and rebuilt again.

```
pristine:            controls 3/3 present   secret strings checked: 135   leaked: 0
defect re-injected:  controls 3/3 present   secret strings checked: 135   leaked: 122
```

122 of 135 — the whole email, the reply's prose, the stems, the options and the explanations,
readable in `view-source`. The probe sees a leak when there is one. **The zero is a real
zero.** The harness's own conjunction also failed on the injected defect (`FAIL the reading
route never hands the runner a resolved set`), so the gate and the measurement agree.

Stated rather than implied, as plan 05 stated it: the bank *is* present in exactly one
`.next/static/chunks/*.js` file. That is the posture T-02.1-41 accepts — the same one the
shipped practice rooms have — and claiming otherwise would be worse than the defect.

### The mutation that lied, and why it is written down

The first batch reported one survivor: "a blank loses its explanation". It was not a gate gap.
The mutation replaced the *opening clause* of the explanation string with a quote character,
which left `", and it is the figure the first email gives you first…"` — a **still non-empty
explanation**. The gate was right; the mutation had not done what its own label claimed.

Rewritten to actually empty the field (`explanation: ""`, the original moved to a dead key),
plus two siblings — a question explanation emptied, and one reduced to whitespace, to prove
`filled()` trims. All three fail. Recorded because this is hazard #1 pointing the other way:
plan 08's harness reported false *catches*; this one reported a false *survivor*. Both are the
same disease — a mutation you have not verified actually mutates.

## Deviations from Plan

### [Rule 1 — the plan's assertion targets a field that deliberately does not exist] `timeLimitMinutes` became an assertion on the derivation

Task 3 asks the harness to "assert that a set's `timeLimitMinutes` equals the sum of its
parts' `minutes`". **`CelpipReadingSet` has no `timeLimitMinutes` field**, on purpose: plan 08
removed it so the total could not be typed twice and drift. Asserting equality between a
derived value and its own derivation would be a tautology.

- **Fix:** the harness computes the sum itself and asserts `readingSetMinutes(set)` equals it —
  so the *derivation* is what is checked, not a number agreeing with itself. Proved real by
  mutation: `readingSetMinutes` hard-coded to 39 fails. The sibling assertion on
  `readingSetItemCount` fails when it stops counting blanks.
- **Files:** `scripts/verify-celpip-content.mts` · **Commit:** `f3d1f05`

### [Rule 2 — the gate this partial set needed most, and the plan does not name it] Exam order is asserted as a subsequence

The plan says not to assert four-part coverage, and it is right. It says nothing about
**order**, and order is not coverage.

This set ships parts **1 and 3**. Its array therefore has a *hole in the middle*, which is a
shape the listening bank never had. Plan 10's own instruction — and this file's original
header — is "adding a part is a pure append", and a pure append would produce
`[correspondence, information, diagram, viewpoints]`: the exam's part 3 read before its part 2,
under the wrong clock, with `tsc`, lint, the build and every other assertion passing.

- **Fix:** a subsequence assertion against `CELPIP_READING_PART_KINDS`, the same one the
  listening group carries, **plus** a warning in the bank file's header saying in as many words
  that the array is *not* append-only and the diagram must be **inserted**. Caught by mutation.
- **Files:** `scripts/verify-celpip-content.mts`, `src/lib/celpip/reading-set-1.ts`

### [Rule 2] Three gates the plan did not list, all cheap and all silent when broken

- **A declared blank must appear in the text exactly once**, not merely at least once. Twice
  means two selects writing to one answers-map key, the second silently overwriting the first.
- **Blanks declared in reading order.** `DropdownBlank`'s accessible label is its index in
  `blanks`; its position on the page comes from `segments`. Disagree and a screen-reader user
  is told she is filling blank 3 while filling blank 1.
- **No reading question carries a `segmentId`.** The type is shared with Listening. A stray one
  is a copy-paste from the listening bank — the exact move D-07 forbids and this plan rejected
  by name.

### [Judgement call] The reading section carries no caveat

Listening's caveat names synthesised audio; Speaking's names the written-out photograph. Both
describe a **compromise in the thing itself** that no count can express. Reading has none: it
is simply **incomplete**, and its derived line already reads "2 of the 4 exam part shapes" in
numbers that move on their own when plan 10 commits. A hand-written sentence naming the two
missing parts would be stale that same day. The reasoning is in a comment at the call site so a
