# Phase 02.1 Plan 03: Speaking, All Eight Task Shapes Summary

Speaking is finished. All eight real CELPIP task shapes ship with one original prompt each at
the exam's own prep and response timings, the self-evaluation covers all four spoken-performance
axes, and a new content harness proves the bank covers the exam rather than merely type-checking.

All three tasks executed, verified and committed. Every `<verify>` block in the plan ran and
passed. One item is owed and recorded in the ledger; one mutation survived the first pass and
was closed.

## What shipped

### Task 1 — seven more prompts, all eight shapes (commit `ed09837`)

**The eight prompt ids, in exam order** (the record the plan's `<output>` asks for):

The plan-01 prompt is unchanged and is the `advice` entry. Every scenario is 50-70 words in
everyday-Canadian contexts; every prompt carries three strategy tips (four on the plan-01 one)
in the plain-spoken voice `tasks-email.ts` uses. **All prose is original (D-06)** — written from
the exam FORMAT only. The repository-wide third-party-name grep (`prepcelpip`, `celpipace`,
`clbready`, `celpipguide`, `celpip.ca`, `paragon testing`) returns zero matches across `src/`
and `scripts/`.

**The timings are now fact, and the code says so.** `SPEAKING_TASK_TIMINGS` is one exported
lookup keyed by the exam's task number, commented to record that the figures are confirmed
against the learner's official format material and that only the *structure* — task names and
numbers — crossed over. RESEARCH's assumption A2 is closed.

A prompt cannot choose its own windows. `withExamTimings()` takes a seed typed as
`Omit<CelpipSpeakingPrompt, "prepSeconds" | "responseSeconds">` and fills both from the lookup,
throwing loudly on a task number the table does not hold. That is why a mutation that hard-codes
30/60 inside the builder is caught rather than quietly miscalibrating six of the eight prompts.

**Task 3 ships a written scene, and says so three times.** In its own scenario ("In the real exam
this task puts a photograph in front of you… This app has no original photograph to offer, so the
scene is written out below instead"), in a comment beside the prompt recording it as an **open
content dependency** rather than a closed decision, and in the section caveat the landing renders
under the Speaking tab. The comment states the migration path: a real original image needs no
type change beyond an optional `sceneImage` field, because `SpeakingRecorder` already renders
`sceneDescription` as its own paragraph in both the brief and the response view — an image would
slot in beside it, with the written scene surviving as the accessible alternative.

The landing needed no edit to report the new prompts: the coverage line's numerator and the card
grid both follow `SPEAKING_PROMPTS`. The only change to `src/lib/celpip.ts` is the caveat string.

### Task 2 — the remaining three rubric dimensions (commit `a08ea45`)

Four dimensions now, mirroring the axes the official descriptors use for **spoken** performance
rather than the writing ones: **Task Fulfilment** (shipped in plan 01), **Content & Coherence**,
**Vocabulary**, **Listenability**. Four items each, sixteen in total.

The source was `CELPIP-Level-Descriptors-Dec2023.pdf` from the beta user's own archive, handled
exactly as Phase 1 handled the Writing rubric: **read for meaning, then written from scratch.**
Only the four axis names crossed over, and they are labels for what is being judged, in the same
category as task names and timings. Nothing is quoted and nothing is lightly reworded — the
descriptors' own phrasings ("self-corrections, repetitions, or inappropriate pauses",
"intelligibly with fluent rhythm") appear nowhere in the file.

Every question is answerable **while listening back to a sixty-second recording**, which is the
constraint that decides whether a self-check is real or decorative:

- *"Listening back, is there one word you reached for over and over — 'good', 'thing', 'nice', 'a lot'?"* — answerable.
- *"Did the ends of your words survive at full speed — plurals, past tenses, the third-person 's'?"* — answerable.
- *"Was your vocabulary at a high level?"* — the item that was deliberately not written.

Every item carries **both** a `text` question and an `explanation` (`RubricChecklist` renders the
explanation unconditionally, so an item without one is a visible gap). Every id keeps the
`speaking-` prefix, and all 34 rubric ids across email, survey and speaking are unique. **No
check-all gate** was added anywhere; a partly-ticked list stays a valid, persisted result.

### Task 3 — `scripts/verify-celpip-content.mts` (commit `af88b1b`, hardened in `3c6afc3`)

The Wave 0 validation gap RESEARCH flagged: `tsc` proves a prompt has the right *fields* and says
nothing about whether the bank covers the exam. **88 assertions**, in the established posture —
plain node under `--experimental-strip-types`, no test runner, no new dependency, `.mts`, explicit
`.ts` import extensions, `ok` / `deepEqual` / `group` helpers, a failure counter and
`process.exit(1)`.

What it proves, in five groups:

- **Coverage** — eight prompts for eight shapes, each shape covered exactly once, no shape outside
  the union, unique non-empty ids, `getSpeakingPrompt` resolving every id and rejecting an unknown
  one, task numbers 1-8 with no repeats.
- **Completeness** — every prompt has a title, scenario text and at least one non-empty strategy
  tip; both windows are sane integers (prep 15-120s, response 30-180s).
- **Timings** — the lookup's sixteen numbers pinned by rule (tasks 5 and 6 get the long prep;
  tasks 1 and 7 get the long response; everything else 30/60), plus the invariant that every
  prompt's pair came from the lookup for its own task number.
- **The Task 3 substitution (T-02.1-14)** — the scene exists, no other shape carries one, and the
  disclosure appears in both the prompt's copy and the landing's caveat.
- **The rubric (T-02.1-13)** — four dimensions, no duplicate keys, every item with a question and
  an explanation, every id `speaking-` prefixed, and no id reused anywhere across writing and
  speaking. That last one matters because these ids are the keys stored in `checkedRubric`: a
  collision would cross-contaminate one learner's history between two sections, and nothing else
  in the build would notice.

**Structured as a low-conflict append target**, because plans 04 and 07 both land here. Shared
helpers at the top, one marked import block, per-skill sections below, and a header comment
stating in those words that appending is two edits — one import line and one new section at the
bottom — and that the existing groups must not be reorganised to make room.

`package.json` was **not** touched. It wraps none of the five existing verification scripts, so
adding a wrapper for this one alone would have been the disturbance the plan told me to avoid.

## Mutation testing — and the gap the first green run hid

A first green is not evidence. **33 deliberate defects; 32 caught, 1 survived.** Two controls (a
reworded strategy tip, a reworded rubric label) correctly did *not* fail — a gate that fails on
harmless edits gets disabled rather than fixed.

The harness **aborts loudly on a missing OR ambiguous anchor** and reports zero harness errors, so
every result below is from a mutation that was actually applied. That guard is not theoretical:
`.planning/WINDOWS.md` in this very repo has **mixed** line endings (frontmatter CRLF, table and
JSON LF), which is the same class of trap that produced three phantom "gaps" in plan 01. Backups
went to an explicit scratchpad path and were restored in a `finally`; the gate was re-run green on
clean source afterwards.

### Caught (32)

Bank: a shape missing · two prompts claiming one shape · a shape outside the union · duplicate id ·
duplicate task number · empty scenario · whitespace-only title · no strategy tips · task 1's 90s
flattened to 60 · task 7's 90s flattened to 60 · task 5 losing its 60s prep · a 6000-second
response · a zero prep window · `withExamTimings` ignoring the lookup.

Task 3 honesty: the scene deleted · a scene on a shape that never shows one · the whole disclosure
stripped from the prompt · the prompt naming the picture but not the substitution · the prompt
naming the substitution but not the picture · the caveat losing the picture · the caveat losing

Rubric: a whole dimension deleted · a dimension with no items · duplicate dimension key · an empty
explanation · a whitespace-only explanation · an empty question · an id losing its `speaking-`
prefix · a speaking id colliding with `email-precision` · two speaking ids colliding.

### Survived, then closed (1)

**The Task 3 disclosure gate could be defeated by the word "picture".** Deleting the entire
written-scene disclosure from the prompt's scenario left both `verify-celpip-content` and
`verify-celpip-sections` green, because the surviving copy still read *"read it, **picture** it,
and describe it"* — the verb, not the noun, matched `/photograph|picture|image/i`. T-02.1-14's
mitigation was real on the landing and hollow in the prompt.

The check is now a **conjunction**: an image noun with an article in front of it (which the verb
cannot satisfy) **and** a clause saying this app substitutes for it. Three mutations now cover the
halves — noun without substitution, substitution without noun, and the original whole-disclosure
deletion — and all three fail the gate. Committed separately as `3c6afc3` with the finding
recorded in the source comment, so a future reader cannot re-loosen it by accident.

### [Judgement call] The exam timings are pinned, not merely bounded

The threat register dispositions T-02.1-15 (wrong timings) as **accept**, on the reasoning that
the figures came from third-party prep sites and a correction should stay a single edit. That
premise changed: this phase had privileged access to the learner's official format material and
the figures are now confirmed. The harness therefore asserts the sixteen numbers by rule as well
as by bounds, which makes a correction a two-file edit **on purpose** — the risk is no longer
"we guessed and may need to fix it" but "we know, and must not lose it to a careless edit". The
sane-bounds assertions were kept alongside, so a nonsense value fails twice.

### [Judgement call] Task 8 is not a second image task

RESEARCH names only Task 3 as image-based, so Task 8 is authored as a spoken description of
something unusual from the learner's own experience rather than as a second written-scene
stand-in. Inventing a second substitution would have doubled a compromise the phase is trying to
keep to one.

### [Observation, not a deviation] The speaking route is server-rendered on demand

Task 1's `<done>` says "the build pre-renders eight speaking pages". `generateStaticParams` now
returns all eight ids, and `npm run build` lists `ƒ /celpip/speaking/[promptId]` — **exactly as it
lists `ƒ /celpip/writing/[taskId]`**. The segment is dynamic for a reason that predates this phase
and applies equally to the shipped writing route; plan 01 recorded the same observation. No change
was made to chase the static marker.
