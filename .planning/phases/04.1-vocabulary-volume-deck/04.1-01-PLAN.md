Five artifacts, one commit, because none of them is meaningful without the others: a bank with no id
space is unrenderable, an id space with no surface is D-05, and a surface with no committed word
list is a claim about provenance nobody can check.

**1. The committed word list — `scripts/fixtures/ngsl-headwords.tsv`.**

Obtain the New General Service List headwords from the public source. The list itself is public and
citable, and it answers exactly one question for this phase: *which words*. **It supplies no prose.
Every gloss and every example in this app is written here, and that line has held since Phase 1 and
is not relaxed by volume pressure.**

Format: tab-separated, `rank<TAB>headword`, one per line, ranks contiguous from 1, headwords
lowercase and unique, sorted by rank. `#`-prefixed comment lines at the top carry the provenance:
source URL, retrieval date, the licence, and one sentence saying that the list answers *which words*
and contributes no sentence to the app.

It lives in `scripts/fixtures/` beside `scheduled-item-ids.json`, and NOT in `src/`, for a concrete
reason worth writing into the header: it is gate input, not app content. Nothing at runtime needs it,
and putting it under `src/` would bundle several thousand words into a client chunk to answer a
question only the harness asks.

**If the list cannot be obtained, STOP and ask the user.** Do not author a substitute list from
memory and do not derive one from the words you were about to write. A provenance assertion checked
against a list assembled to fit the cards it validates is not a provenance assertion — it is a
mirror, and it would read green forever while proving nothing. This is a stop, not a workaround.

**2. The skip register — `src/lib/content/core-vocabulary-skips.ts`.**

Exports `SKIPPED_HEADWORDS: { word: string; reason: string }[]`. A word from the NGSL that the deck
passes over is declared here with a reason, and the reasons are a small closed vocabulary stated in
the module header: a function word that no flashcard can teach (`the`, `of`, `and`); a word already
taught by a scenario vocabulary card; a word whose base form cannot appear in its own example
(`go`/`went` — see the inflection rule in plan 02).

This register exists so that *which words* stays derived from the NGSL rather than from an author's
taste. Plan 02 gates it: for every rank below the deepest rank the deck has reached, the headword is
either in the deck or in this register. **Silent cherry-picking is the failure mode that would quietly
turn a frequency backbone into five hundred easy-to-example words**, and this phase's entire
justification is frequency coverage.

React-free, alias-free, explicit `.ts` extensions. It is imported by the harness and by nothing else,
so it never enters a client bundle — say so in its header.

**3. The bank — `src/lib/content/core-vocabulary.ts`, per L2.**

```
CoreVocabCard = { id, word, es, example }      // and NOTHING else
```

**The absent `tip` is the design, not an omission, and the header must say so in those words.** This
project's rule is that an optional field is a field an author forgets, so the volume tier's lower bar
is expressed by the type having nowhere to put a register note. A word that genuinely needs one —
who says it, to whom, and what getting it wrong costs — belongs in a scenario bank instead, and the
header should say that too, so the next author has somewhere to put the card rather than a reason to
widen this type.

Rows are authored through one compact helper so a card is one line, and `id` is derived from `word`
by ONE slugifier in ONE place. **This looks like `vocabulary.ts`'s `${deckId}:${i}` and is its
opposite, and the header must draw the distinction explicitly** or a later reader will "fix" it: the
deck browser derives an id from ARRAY POSITION, so inserting a card renumbers every later card and
orphans a learner's progress on all of them. Here the id is derived from the card's own CONTENT,
which does not move when a neighbour is inserted, and which cannot silently duplicate because plan
02 asserts no word repeats. Insert freely; never rename a word under a live id.

The bank is ordered by NGSL rank, ascending. Plan 02 asserts it, and the order is what gives the
surface's bands their meaning.

For this plan, author **twenty cards** from the top of the list, skipping function words into the
skip register as you meet them. They are real cards held to the volume bar, not placeholders:
`es` is a natural Spanish gloss a learner would recognise and not the English word; `example` is one
sentence somebody would actually say, containing the word, at least six words long. **Vary the
opening of every example deliberately** — plan 02 gates a frame-diversity ceiling, and twenty cards
that all open `I have a…` is precisely what "flat" means.

**4. The id space — `src/lib/core-vocab-items.ts`.**

A NEW module, not a section of `review-items.ts`, and the reason is that file's own D-05 header
argument taken seriously rather than copied. Its argument is that **a key space keeps composition and
resolution together** so the two cannot drift into an id that is stored, merged and scheduled
perfectly and never rendered. It is not an argument that all key spaces share one file. Two things
follow, and both favour separation:

- This plan's central promise is that `parseScenarioItemId` and `resolveReviewItem` are unchanged.
  In a separate module that is checkable with `git diff --stat`; inside `review-items.ts` it is a
  matter of care, and care is what a one-way door does not get to depend on.
- The dangerous edge is a *runtime import* between the two spaces, in either direction. Separate
  modules make the absence of that edge a source-level fact plan 02 can assert.

Exports:

- `CORE_VOCAB_PREFIX` and the composer `coreVocabItemId(slug)` → `` `vocab:${slug}` ``. **Nothing
  outside this module spells the format**, exactly as `scenarioItemId` is the sole author of the
  scenario format, and plan 02 asserts it from the bank's source the way the existing one-author group
  already does.
- `parseCoreVocabItemId(id)` → the slug, or `undefined`. Its own parser, with no reference to
  `parseScenarioItemId` and no scenario lookup. Returns nothing for anything that is not exactly
  `vocab:` + a non-empty slug the bank actually holds.
- `coreVocabRecallItems()` → `RecallItem[]` for the whole bank, and `resolveCoreVocabItem(id)` →
  one `RecallItem` or `undefined`. `RecallItem` arrives by a **type-only import** from
  `review-items.ts` — erased at build, so it creates no runtime edge, and plan 02 asserts the import
  is type-only rather than trusting it.
  `front` is the Spanish, `back` is the word, `hint` is the example, `level` is the deck's declared
  level and `xpSkill` is `reading`, matching the convention the scenario vocabulary already uses.
  `topic` is the single exported constant `CORE_VOCAB_TOPIC` — one string for the whole tier, because
  `recordAttempt` stores `topic` and `weakTopics()` groups by it, and Task 2 depends on that string
  being one recognisable value rather than five hundred.
- `coreVocabIds()` → every id the bank can emit. **This is the volume tier's own enumerator and it is
  NOT `reviewableIds()`.** L1 said this bank must be listed in `reviewableIds()` because it is scored;
  L5 supersedes that, and the header must record the supersession and the symmetric cost that makes it
  worth writing down: omit a scored bank from the enumerator its own surface uses and its cards are
  invisible; add it to the shared one and it floods `/review`. This module is the first case in the
  app where those are two different enumerators, so the header states which is which.
- `coreVocabBands()` → the study bands, computed as `recallBatches(cards, CORE_VOCAB_BAND_SIZE)` with
  the ceiling at 50. **Do not write banding arithmetic here.** `recall-batches.ts` is the ONE author of
  a split in this app, its properties are already gated over lengths 0–60 and all 35 real decks, and
  reusing it means a band inherits every one of them — no empty band, concatenation reproduces the
  bank in order, and the last band is never a stub.

The module header carries the D-05 argument in its own words, the supersession above, and the fact
that the `:` is the collision property the way `/` was for the scenario space in Phase 3 — **asserted
in plan 02, not trusted**, and asserted against all four existing spaces rather than the two L1 names.
There is a fourth space with a colon already: the deck browser's card ids are `${deckId}:${i}`, so
`daily:0` exists today. The `vocab:` prefix is safe only because no deck is named `vocab`, and that is
a fact to gate, not to remember.
