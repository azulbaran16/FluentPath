# 04.1-09 — the sense-consistency pass over the 463 untouched cards

**Not a plan.** Follow-up work the user asked for after `04.1-08`, whose executor found a defect
class the gate's reader pass never named — gloss and example naming different Spanish senses —
and measured it at 3 in 37 while saying plainly that *the other 463 have never been checked*.

## How the 463 were read

Four **independent, read-only** readers over disjoint chunks (116/116/116/115). Each translated
every example into natural Spanish and tested whether the verb or noun a competent translator
actually reaches for matches the gloss. None could write to the repo.

Three classes were asked for, because they are one family — the front and the back not
corresponding — and separating them would have cost four reads of the deck to find one.

**62 findings of 463 = 13.4 %.** With `04.1-08`'s 37, the deck shipped **~99 defective cards of
500 — one in five.** The 8 % extrapolated from a 37-card sample was short by half.

| SENSE-MISMATCH | 5 | 6 | 5 | 8 | **24** |
| TWO-SENSE | 10 | 10 | 0 | 0 | **20** |

### Three things visible only with all four reads side by side

1. **TWO-SENSE is confined to the high-frequency head.** All 20 in chunks 1–2, **zero** below —
   because no gloss down there carries a slash at all. The most frequent words are the most
   polysemous, so the class extinguishes itself as rank deepens. It is not a spread defect.
2. **WRONG-KEY has one cause, and it is a strategy rather than a slip:** re-glossing to avoid a
   front another card already took. `big` held *grande*, so `large` became "amplio / extenso" — a
   front answered with *wide*. Same for `difficult`→"complicado" (*complicated*),
   `role`→"función" (*function*), `policy`→"normativa" (*regulations*).
3. **A cross-card collision no single-card read could see.** `perhaps` fronted "quizás" and
   `maybe` fronted "a lo mejor" — but *quizás*→**maybe** and *a lo mejor*→**perhaps** are both
   correct, so **neither card could ever mark an answer wrong.** A defect in the pair, not in
   either card, and unfixable one-sidedly.

## THE RULE CHANGE — for whoever authors next

**When a front is already taken, disambiguate with a parenthetical, or skip the word. Never
re-gloss onto a different sense.** The parenthetical is a device this deck already uses well —
`morning` "mañana (antes del mediodía)", `pass` "aprobar (un examen)". Re-glossing buys a card
and pays for it with a card that asks one thing and teaches another.

This supersedes the softer line written into the `cognate` decision on 2026-08-05, which said
"if a natural synonym exists, card it" and cited `idea`→"ocurrencia" as an honest gloss. It was
not: it is one of the eighteen. The rule produced the class.

## What changed — 62 cards, `es` and `example` only

`word` and `id` untouched on every one. Representative, not exhaustive; the diff is the record.

**SENSE-MISMATCH** — example rewritten to the gloss's sense, or gloss corrected to the headword's
central sense: `past` (gloss was the noun, example the preposition — the worst drift found),
`poor` (*mala* iluminación → a poverty example), `wish` (*ojalá* → the transitive sense),
`private` (*clases particulares*), `give` (light-verb *llámame*), `serve` (*atender*),
`attack` (noun gloss, verb example), `drop` (*se le cayó* → deliberate *soltar*), `stop`
(*dejar de* → *parar*), `remain` (*quedan* → *permanecer*), `able`, `try`, `become`,
`condition`→"estado (en que algo se encuentra)", `practice`, `step`, `event`, `nice`,
`development`, `recently`, `store`, `fact`, `take`, `expect`→"esperar (que alguien haga algo)".

**TWO-SENSE** — gloss narrowed to the sense the example teaches, parenthetical where it helps:
`know`→"saber (algo)", `work`→"trabajo (empleo)", `then`→"luego (después)",
`keep`→"guardar (conservar)", `care`→"cuidado (atención)", `power`→"corriente (eléctrica)",
`test`→"análisis (médico)", plus `think`, `child`, `change`, `ask`, `end`, `move`, `order`,
`love`, `support`, `cause`, `visit`, `clear`, `tell`. **`plan` was worse than the rest** — neither
glossed option was what its example used — so both sides moved.

**WRONG-KEY** — parentheticals, never re-glosses: `difficult`→"difícil", `role`→"papel (que algo
desempeña)", `policy`→"política (de una empresa)", `large`→"grande (de tamaño o cantidad)",
`late`→"tarde (con retraso)", `kind`→"tipo (de cosa)", `allow`→"permitir", `right`→"acertado",
`certainly`→"sin duda", `discuss`→"hablar de", `major`→"grave / importante",
`single`→"ni un solo (enfático)", `due`, `hope`→"esperar (con ilusión)". `game` moved off
*partido* to "juego" **with a new example**, because the deck's own `match` card uses *match* in
its example and the two fronts were competing.

The `perhaps`/`maybe` pair was fixed **as a pair**, by register: "quizás (más formal)" and
"a lo mejor (coloquial)".

## Two rewrites the checks caught, and that is the point

- **`become`** was first written *"He became a teacher…"*. `became` is an irregular past that does
  not contain its headword as a prefix, so the floor check rejected it. Rewritten to a base-form
  example that still takes a noun complement.
- **`try`** was first written *"Try to arrive before the doors close."*, which collided with
  `focus`'s *"Try to focus on one chapter at a time."* The harness's repeated-opening-shape line —
  **the warning signal 04.1-07 commented precisely because nobody was told to read it** — printed
  `"try to" x2`. Rewritten. It earned its keep a third time.

`main` was 27 commits ahead of `origin/main` and `https://fluenthapp.com/core-vocabulary`

## NOT fixed — reported instead, because each changes the id set

The user chose an in-place rewrite specifically to avoid id-set churn, so nothing was removed.

1. **`idea` should have been a `cognate` skip and is still wrong.** Spanish for *idea* is *idea*;
   there is no honest differing gloss, which is the definition of the `cognate` reason. It sits on
   "ocurrencia" — a whim or a witty remark — and is answered with *notion*. Left as it is.
2. **`large` / `big` and `perhaps` / `maybe` are genuine synonym pairs.** Parentheticals soften
   them; they do not separate them. The honest fix is to skip one of each.
3. **`kind` → "tipo (de cosa)"** still pulls *type* before *kind*. Same shape, lower severity.

## What no gate can see, and now has a name

The whole of this pass. **No assertion in the harness can compare a Spanish gloss to an English
example** — that needs bilingual judgement, and it found a defect in one card of five that
21,914 assertions, four near-duplicate scans and a 112-card reader pass all rated green.

Recorded so it is checked by somebody rather than by nobody: **a batch's cards must be read
against their glosses by a reader, at draft time, while a rewrite is still free.** Add it beside

taken front.
