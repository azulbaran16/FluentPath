<objective>
The gates, landed second and before a single card is authored at volume — the same ordering 04-01
used and for the same reason: the window a gate protects has to open before the risky work starts,
not after it. This phase authors 480 more permanent spaced-repetition keys after this plan. Every one
of them lands inside the detector rather than outside it.

Three things land here that nothing else in this project can do yet:

1. **`verify-id-stability.mts` learns a second key space.** It has hashed scenario ids since 04-01;
   its scope loop currently asserts that every id it holds is a scheduled *scenario* kind, which a
   `vocab:` id is not. Generalising that loop is the delicate edit in this plan, and it must be
   generalised without weakening what it already proves.
2. **The quality floor becomes mechanical**, including the one assertion that addresses the actual
   risk. The existing corpus scan sees repeated words; it cannot see five hundred examples that all
   open the same way, and at five hundred nobody is going to read them all. That is what the
   frame-diversity ceiling is for, and it is the volume tier's automated equivalent of the
   consecutive read that caught repeated syntactic frames in Phases 3 and 4.

   `reviewableIds()`. Under L5 the volume ids are deliberately not in `reviewableIds()` — and they
   *are* in `srs`. Left alone, the gate would keep printing a comfortable figure while the real
   payload grew underneath it, which is the precise shape of failure this project keeps writing
   gates to prevent.

**If execution stops right after this plan**, twenty cards ship with the full machinery around them
and nothing is unprotected. The phase can be resumed, abandoned or re-scoped without a single id
having been created outside a gate.

**A. The quality floor** — six assertions plus their arithmetic, over every card in the bank:

   the next author will widen the matcher instead.

   scenario vocabulary `term`s. Both are assertions.

   Those decks hold high-frequency words on purpose (`actually`, `maybe`, `because`), they are a

   skip words it exists to teach. Printing it keeps the figure visible without turning a legacy deck

   beside five assertions reads as an assertion someone forgot to write.

   token, take the first two tokens, join with a space. Then, with `n` the deck's length:

   - no single opening word holds more than `max(3, floor(n * 0.20))` — because a bigram ceiling

     ceiling cannot see.
