<objective>
The first volume batch: 120 cards, taking the deck from 20 to **140**.

This plan and the three after it are deliberately the same shape, and the shape is the point. Each
authors a contiguous run of NGSL headwords, declares its skips, regenerates the id fixture in the same
commit, re-measures the payload from the harness, and hands the next plan a histogram of how the
deck's example openings are distributed. **Nothing here is discovered at the end.** The gates that
would catch a flat batch, a duplicate word, an off-list word or a payload crossing landed in plan 02,
before a single one of these ids existed.

**Why 120 and not 500 in one go.** Not context anxiety — measurement points. The payload rule is a
stop condition, and a stop condition checked once at the end is not a stop condition. Four batches
means four printed figures on the way to 32.6 %, and if any of them comes in above the projection the
phase stops there with the smaller number of permanent keys already committed.

**If execution stops right after this plan**, the learner has 140 core words on a surface that says
140 because it counts them. Nothing claims five hundred. Every gate is green and every id is recorded.

Purpose: the first real volume, authored inside the machine plan 02 proved.
Output: 120 cards, a regenerated fixture, a payload figure and a histogram.
</objective>

Continue the deck from where plan 01 left it, in NGSL rank order, until it holds **140 cards**.

**Where the words come from, and where they do not.** The NGSL answers *which words*, from the
committed `scripts/fixtures/ngsl-headwords.tsv`, in rank order, skipping nothing silently. **Every
gloss and every example is written here.** No sentence of third-party study material enters this app;
that line has held since Phase 1 and it is not relaxed by volume pressure. If a word's usage is
unfamiliar, look it up and then write your own sentence — do not transcribe one.

**Skips are declared, not taken.** A headword the deck passes over goes into `SKIPPED_HEADWORDS` with
one of the register's reasons: a function word no flashcard can teach; a word a scenario vocabulary
card already teaches; a word whose base form cannot appear in its own example. The harness asserts
completeness below the deepest rank reached, so a silent skip is a failure, not a shortcut. Expect
this batch to carry the heaviest skip load of the four — the top of a frequency list is where the
function words are.

**Each card, held to the volume bar:**

- `word` is the NGSL headword, lowercase, exactly as the list spells it.
- `es` is a natural Spanish gloss a learner would recognise. Not the English word, not a word-for-word
  rendering, and not a dictionary string of five near-synonyms. It is the **front** of a recall card,
  so two cards with near-identical fronts expecting different answers is a usability defect, not a
  stylistic echo — check the front against its neighbours as you write.
- `example` is one sentence somebody would actually say, at least six words, containing the word in a
  form the inflection rule accepts. If the headword's own form genuinely cannot appear, the word goes
  to the skip register — **do not soften the rule and do not reach for the passive to satisfy it**.
- No `tip`. There is nowhere to put one, by design. A word that needs a register note belongs in a
  scenario bank.

**Vary the openings deliberately, and check as you go rather than at the end.** The harness caps any
opening bigram at ~5 % of the deck and any single opening word at 20 %, and prints the top ten. A
batch of 120 written in one sitting is exactly where a house style sets in — every example opening
`I`, or `She`, or `The` — and the gate will catch it, but catching it after 120 cards means rewriting
them, and rewriting an example is only free before the id is committed. Aim for a spread while
writing; run the harness in the middle of the batch, not only at the end.

**Ids are permanent from the moment they commit.** The slug is derived from the word, so renaming a
word under a live id is a re-point and the gate will refuse it. Insert freely; never rename. A
replacement is always: retire the old id in the fixture with a reason, add a new one.

**Regenerate the fixture in THIS commit.** 120 new ids land here and rule 3 fails on any of them that
is not recorded. Run the update mode once, **after the content is final**, read the diff, and confirm
it is additions only with no changed hash on any pre-existing id. Do not run `--update` to make a
failure go away without reading it: it refuses to launder a changed hash and it cannot tell an
uncommitted rewrite from a real re-point. 04-07 tripped exactly this; the recovery is to restore the
fixture from `HEAD` and regenerate once.

**If the printed share is above 40 % of the cap, STOP.** Do not author another card, do not raise the
ceiling, do not "finish the batch first". Report the figure to the user and ask. The projection says
this batch lands near 24 % and the full 500 near 32.6 %, so a reading above 40 % here means a modelling
assumption is wrong and every number after it is untrustworthy. That is exactly the moment worth
stopping at, and the cap does not fail gracefully: above it the route answers 413, `sync-queue`
classifies 413 as permanent and drops the slot, and the learner's progress silently stops syncing.

**2. Record the opening-signature histogram.** Copy the harness's printed top ten into the summary
with counts. The next batch reads it before it writes; that is the whole reason it is printed.

**3. Run the near-duplicate scan the harness cannot do.** The gates catch byte-identity; a lazy
paraphrase passes everything, and that is the named residual risk of every content phase in this
project. Scan this batch's `es` glosses and `example` sentences — exact matches and Jaccard over word
sets — against each other and against the whole existing corpus: the 280 scenario vocabulary cards,
the 210+ scenario phrases, and the deck-browser cards. Record the highest pair in each field.

Two thresholds are worth acting on rather than merely noting. A gloss pair above **0.60** is a
usability defect, because `es` is the **front** of the card and two near-identical fronts expect
different answers. An example pair above **0.50** inside this batch means a frame the signature check
did not catch, because it repeats past the first two words.

**Fix by rewriting, and rewrite BEFORE the ids commit.** Every card in this batch is uncommitted at
the moment the scan runs, so a rewrite is free. After the commit it is a re-point, and the only legal
move becomes retire-and-re-add. That asymmetry is why this task sits inside the same plan as the
authoring rather than in the phase gate.

**4. If anything was rewritten, regenerate the fixture again — once — and re-run every gate.** The
fixture must reflect the content as committed, not as first drafted.
