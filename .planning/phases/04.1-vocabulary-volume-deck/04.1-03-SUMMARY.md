## The opening-signature histogram at 140 cards — read this before writing plan 04

The harness's own printed lines:

```
  volume deck: 140 card(s), deepest NGSL rank 238, 139 distinct opening shape(s);
               ceilings 7 per shape / 28 per opening word
               — 3 word(s) also in the deck browser (book, kind, order), reported not asserted
  top openings: "give me" x2, "a group" x1, "a small" x1, "an old" x1, "an older" x1,
                "are you" x1, "ask the" x1, "at this" x1, "both children" x1, "bring the" x1
```

**139 distinct shapes over 140 cards.** Exactly one bigram repeats — `"give me" x2`,
against a ceiling of 7. The printed top ten is nearly all ties at 1, which makes it
almost useless as a pressure signal, so here is the figure that actually constrains
plan 04 — the **opening WORD** histogram over the whole deck, every count ≥ 2:

```
the x12, they x5, my x4, give x3, her x3, our x3, she x3, that x3, we x3,
a x2, an x2, can x2, his x2, i x2, in x2, kids x2, let's x2, nobody x2,
nothing x2, one x2, prices x2, somebody x2, there x2, this x2, what x2, your x2
distinct opening words: 93 of 140
```

**Where the pressure is, stated plainly for the next author:**

- `the` at **12** is the only opener above 5, against a ceiling of 28 at n=140 and
  52 at n=260. It has room, but it is the one that compounds: a batch that opens a
  third of its examples with `the` would put the deck near 50 and the ceiling only
  reaches 52 at n=260. **Budget `the` at no more than one opening in ten.**
- `they`, `my`, `our`, `her`, `his`, `she`, `we`, `i` — the pronoun/possessive
  openers total **25 of 140**. They are the house style that sets in when you write
  fast; they are also individually small. Keep them there.
- The bigram ceiling is far from binding (worst is 2 of 7) and will stay so if
  openings vary at all. **The opening WORD ceiling is the one plan 04 can actually
  trip**, and it is the one nobody looks at.
- At n=260 the ceilings become **13 per shape / 52 per opening word / 52 distinct
  shapes**; at n=500, **25 / 100 / 100**.

## Things the plan or the gates got wrong

1. **The plan's own task order contradicts its own rule about `--update`, and
   following it literally regenerates the fixture twice.** Task 1 says author and
   regenerate; task 2 then runs the near-duplicate scan, rewrites anything over
   threshold, and says "regenerate the fixture again — once". But the rule that
   governs this file, repeated in CONTEXT, AGENTS.md and this plan's own action
   text, is *`--update` runs **once**, after the content is final*. Content is not
   final until the scan has run. **The scan was therefore run first and the fixture
   regenerated once.** Nothing was harmed either way — the fixture is uncommitted
   between the two — but plans 04, 05 and 06 carry the same task order and should
   invert it, because "run it twice and it's fine because it isn't committed yet"
   is exactly the habit the 04-07 incident came from.

2. **The plan's task-1 idempotence check fails by design if run in the listed
   order** — the same defect plan 02's summary recorded as its item 3, still
   present in this plan verbatim. `BEFORE=hash; --update; AFTER=hash; test equal`
   is an **idempotence** check and is only meaningful *after* the regeneration. Run
   before it, `--update` legitimately rewrites the fixture and the check reports a
   failure that is the tool working.

3. **The harness's printed "top ten openings" is nearly useless as the pressure
   signal it is meant to be**, and this is the first batch large enough to show it.
   It ranks by **bigram**, and bigrams are almost all ties at 1 (139 distinct over
   140 cards), so the printed list is ten alphabetically-early singletons. The
   number that constrains an author is the **opening-word** count, which the
   harness computes (it asserts a ceiling on it) and does **not** print. A future
   plan should print the top ten openers beside the top ten shapes; until then,
   the histogram in this summary is where plan 04 has to get it.

4. **The deck-browser overlap grew from 0 to 3** (`book`, `kind`, `order`) and is
   still *reported, not asserted*, exactly as plan 02 designed. That was the right
   call and it should not now be quietly reconsidered: those decks hold
   high-frequency words on purpose. But the figure will keep climbing through plans
   04–06 and somebody will eventually read a growing unasserted number as a
   problem. It is not one. It is the two products overlapping where they were
   always going to.

5. **The distinct-shape floor is still dominated at n=140**, as plan 02 predicted
   it would be until roughly n≥40 in theory — in practice 139 distinct shapes
   against a floor of 28 means it has never been near firing. It becomes a real
   assertion only if a batch is written flat, which is the case it exists for.
   Plan 07 should keep expecting the bigram ceiling to fire first.
