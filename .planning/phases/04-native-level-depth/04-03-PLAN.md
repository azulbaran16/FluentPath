<objective>
This is the phase's headline quality decision and the only scenario where the existing content has
to be **re-selected rather than extended**. Phase 3's own gate flagged it unprompted — original,
yes; *ours*, not really — and research explains why volume alone cannot fix it: the fourteen items
are almost exactly the classic core-idiom list, which corpus linguistics has repeatedly shown to be
the *rarest* phraseology in English. Twenty more canonical idioms would make the bank longer and no
more native.

So the change of criterion is the work: from opaque-and-teachable to frequency-attested-and-non-
transparent, and from an expression to an expression-in-a-sense-in-a-register. The bar already
exists inside this scenario — its own reading passage turns on exactly that class of expression —
it just is not in the phrase bank.

Retiring an item is safe for the data and irreversible for the learner: the stored entry is simply
skipped, but her schedule on it is gone and cannot be handed to a replacement without re-pointing an
id, which is forbidden. So the retirement list is a human decision before it is a commit.

**If execution stops right after this plan**, the scenario's warm-up is the phase's best content and
its deck is still Phase 3's — visibly inconsistent to a reader, but not a lie: the page's counts are
derived, so it claims exactly what it holds. 04-04 closes the deck and the briefing behind it.

Purpose: fix a selection failure, not a volume one.
Output: 18 re-selected phrases, an approved and recorded retirement list, and a gate on the tips.
</objective>

    Which of the fourteen items `native/idioms` holds today (six phrases, eight vocabulary cards)
    are retired, and which are kept.

  <context>
Before presenting anything, do the work the decision rests on. Read the scenario's six phrases, its
eight cards, its briefing and its reading passage, and produce a table with one row per item:
the item as it reads today, whether the tip it carries is a register-and-use note or a translation,
whether the expression is of the class research names as frequent and non-transparent or of the
class it names as rarest, whether the item is already duplicated on another surface of this same
scenario, and a recommendation of keep or retire with a one-line reason.

Then state the consequences plainly, because they are what the decision is actually about:

- **Retiring is safe for the store and lossy for the learner.** The stored entry goes unreferenced
  and the review screen skips it — no migration, no crash, no corruption. What is gone is her
  schedule on that item. The beta user may have studied some of these.
- **Re-pointing is what must never happen.** Reusing an existing id for different content silently
  transfers her box and her due date onto material she has never seen, with no way to detect it
  afterwards. So a replacement is always: delete the old id, add a new one with its own slug. There
  is no third option, and this is not a preference.
- **The floors bind.** The bank may not fall below six phrases or eight cards at any point, and this
  plan is taking it to eighteen anyway, so retirement never leaves the scenario thin.
- **Retiring nothing is a legitimate answer.** It keeps every learner's schedule intact and leaves
  the canonical list sitting underneath eighteen better items as a beginner layer. It is not the
  recommendation, but it costs her nothing and it should be on the table.

Present the table, the recommendation, and the count of ids each option would retire.

  <action>
Apply the approved retirement list exactly as approved — no more, no less — by deleting those
entries from the `native/idioms` set, and record each deleted id in the id-stability fixture's
retired list with the one-line reason from Task 1's table. An un-declared deletion fails the gate,
which is the point: a retirement is auditable rather than merely survivable.

Then bring the set to eighteen phrases. The additions are authored against the criteria, item by
item, and the criteria are what make this native-level rather than merely longer:

- **Frequency-attested, not dictionary-attested.** The expression earns its slot because a native
  speaker would produce it this month. The class to aim at is the one this scenario's own reading
  passage already turns on — ordinary-looking, non-transparent, and doing real work in a sentence.
- **Non-transparent but decomposable.** The pedagogically load-bearing class is the mundane
  discourse formula whose meaning is not the sum of its words but whose parts still help. Fully
  opaque set-piece idioms are the rarest class in English and are what this bank is being rescued
  from; a replacement drawn from the same class is not a replacement.
- **Register-marked, and the mark is part of the item.** Every one of the eighteen carries a `tip`
  that says who says it, to whom, and what it costs to get that wrong. A tip that translates the
  expression does not count and will fail the gate in Task 3. The scenario next door already does
  this correctly and is the model.
- **Chosen because a C1 learner gets that particular thing wrong**, which for idiom is over-use and
  mis-placement: deploying one where a plain sentence belongs. The scenario's own rehearsal already
  teaches exactly that, and its briefing says learn a few well rather than many badly. Eighteen
  items must not contradict that advice — so let a share of them be expressions whose value is that
  they are unremarkable, and let the tips carry the restraint the briefing asks for.

Two hard exclusions:

- **Do not put into this bank anything the scenario's reading passage asks the reader to recover.**
  That passage deliberately keeps its glossary silent on the four expressions its questions turn on,
  so that the learner has to reconstruct them from context — which is the actual skill. Glossing one
  of them here converts a reading exercise into a memorised definition. Read the passage's header
  comment first; it names them.
- **Do not repeat anything the scenario's own deck or briefing already teaches.** Those surfaces are
  04-04's and this task must leave them a clean field.

Every addition gets a NEW authored slug. Do not edit or renumber a surviving id: whatever Task 1
kept, keeps its content byte for byte.

Then raise `minutes` for `native/idioms` from 10 to the smallest whole minute the session-length
invariant allows for eighteen warm-up phrases and the deck as it stands after this commit.

Before committing, run a corpus-wide exact and Jaccard scan of the new phrase texts against all 35
scenarios and record the highest pair.
