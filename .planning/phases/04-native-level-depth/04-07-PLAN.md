<objective>
The honest one. Research is blunt about this scenario: it is the cheapest of the five in words and
the **lowest value per word**, because the drills pronunciation actually needs — minimal pairs as an
exercise, word stress as an exercise, intonation as an exercise — are precisely the new drill
components D-01 rejected. Its warm-up slot holds tongue-twisters, which the repo's own comment calls
a legitimate shape here and a poor model for anything else, and its deck is metalanguage rather than
practice.

So this plan does the opposite of padding. `native/pronunciation` is taken to **12 phrases and 16
cards** — half again, not three times — and the phase says why, in the banks and in the record,
rather than presenting a uniform floor that would imply the five scenarios were equally deepened when
they were not. Under D-02's spirit, a phase that pads a weak surface to match a strong one is making
a claim with content instead of with words.

There is no honest new skill declaration available here either: reading and writing do not train
pronunciation, and a grammar question about stress placement would be a drill component wearing a
quiz's clothing, which is the line D-01 draws. That is stated here so the declarations decision at
04-08 does not have to re-derive it.

**If execution stops right after this plan**, all five native scenarios carry depth, four of them
deeply and this one deliberately less, and the record says which is which.

Purpose: real depth in the shape that exists, and an honest account of its ceiling.
Output: six warm-up lines that are real utterances, eight more terms of metalanguage, and a written reason.
</objective>

Take the set from six to twelve. **Do not add six more tongue-twisters.** The existing five are a
legitimate shape for a warm-up here and the repo already says they are a poor model for anything
else; six more would be volume with no gain, which is the padding this plan refuses.

The six additions follow the shape of the one item in the set that already works: **the contrast
sits inside a sentence a person would really say to another person**, so the recogniser has a real
word to get right or wrong and the learner gets a score that means something. Cover the contrasts a
Spanish speaker actually loses meaning on — the short and long vowel pair, the two dental fricatives
against their nearest neighbours, initial consonant clusters that invite an inserted vowel, the
voiced and voiceless final consonants that carry a grammatical distinction, and the two syllable
stress positions that turn a noun into a verb — and put each one where getting it wrong would
genuinely change what the listener understood.

Writing a sentence that happens to contain a contrast is **not** building a minimal-pairs component:
the deferred item under D-01 is a new drill and a new renderer, and this adds neither. The item this
follows is already in the bank. Say so in the header comment so the next reader does not mistake one
for the other.

Every one of the six carries a `tip` that names what the mouth does, not what the phrase means — the
existing tips are the model — and the `es` field says which contrast the line is for, in the
parenthetical style the set already uses, rather than pretending a tongue-twister has a translation.

New authored slugs only; the six existing entries are untouched.

depth that is not there. Name the deferred premium-voice work as the thing that would change that

Take the deck from eight cards to sixteen. The rule stands: this deck is the vocabulary a learner

feedback loop this scenario has, and eight more terms materially widen it.

Cover the working set the existing eight leave out: the schwa and why unstressed syllables collapse
into it, weak forms, linking and elision across word boundaries, aspiration, the voiced/voiceless
distinction, sentence stress as against word stress, the rising and falling contours and what each

thing a C1 learner can be told, because it tells her which of her features to spend effort on and
which to keep.
