What the ten drill, drawn from the three C1 production failures research names:

- **The particle, not the verb.** The particle is what carries the meaning and the frequent verbs
  are heavily polysemous. Build distractors out of real sibling verbs — the wrong particle must
  produce a real English verb with a different meaning, not a non-word, so getting it wrong is
  choosing the wrong meaning rather than choosing nonsense. The existing drag and make items are the
  model; do not repeat either family.
- **The sense.** Where a verb the scenario teaches carries two dominant senses, ask which one the
  surrounding sentence forces. The explanation names both senses and says what in the sentence
  decides between them.
- **The register collision.** Producing a formal single-word verb in casual speech, or a phrasal
  verb in a contract. The scenario already has exactly one item that drills this directly and it is
  the only one of its kind in the world; this is where that becomes a strand rather than a curiosity.
- **Fixedness and separability**, extending rather than repeating what the two existing separability
  items do: a pronoun object forcing the split, an inseparable verb refusing it, and — new — a form
  that is simply fixed, where the learner's instinct to vary it is what fails.

Authoring rules, all of them load-bearing:

- The `topic` string is a stable identifier. `recordAttempt` stores it and `weakTopics()` groups a
  learner's history by that exact string, so reusing one of the scenario's three existing strings
  makes her history aggregate and inventing a near-variant of one splits it in two, permanently and
  undetectably. Copy an existing string character for character, or introduce at most one genuinely
  new string and treat it as permanent from this commit.
- Each question stores an authored `slug`, unique within the scenario, and never the composed id —
  `getScenarioGrammar` composes it through the one author of that format on the way out, and the
  harness asserts at the source that this bank never spells the format by hand.
- `grammar` is a scheduled kind, so every slug here becomes a key in live learner data. New slugs
  only. Do not edit, renumber or re-point an existing one.
- Every question needs a filled `explain` that says why the answer is the answer, written as
  substantive prose rather than to a formula. It is the only teaching the learner gets after she
  answers, and 03-11 measured that this corpus's explanations are deliberately not formulaic.
- Nothing here re-uses material from the scenario's briefing, its 18 phrases or its 24 cards. Read
  all three first.

**Regenerate the id fixture in this same commit.** Ten new scheduled ids land here, and the gate
