`native/register` is, by research's reading, the highest-yield-per-word scenario in the world: its
writing task is the strongest prompt in the corpus, its deck already marks register correctly, and
its phrase set is the only one in the app deliberately built as contrasting pairs — the same
situation said casually and formally, because a dial cannot be shown with one line.

Depth here means more situations on the dial, not more lines about tone. Three situations become
nine, and the deck grows from the five markers it names to the full working set a C1 learner needs
in order to *say what changed* rather than merely feel that something did.

other two truthfully report Phase 3's floors. The learner gets the scenario that most directly
answers "why does my English sound wrong in this room" carrying nine worked contrasts.

Output: nine contrasting phrase pairs, a 24-card marker deck, and a gate on the pairing.

  <files>src/lib/content/phrases.ts, src/lib/curriculum.ts, scripts/fixtures/scheduled-item-ids.json</files>
  <read_first>
    The existing `native/register` set and its header comment, which states the pairing rule and why
    a flat list cannot show a dial. The scenario's writing task in `scenario-writing.ts` — its
    checklist is made entirely of *differences*, and those differences are the syllabus this phrase
    set should cover. RESEARCH §1 criterion 3 on the mark being part of the item.
  </read_first>
  <action>
Take the set from six phrases to eighteen, keeping the shape the scenario already has and the header
comment already states: **three contrasting pairs become nine.** Each pair is one situation said
twice — once casually, once formally — with the same facts in both halves, because softening the
content out of the polite version is the real-world failure this scenario exists to prevent, and the
writing task's checklist says so already.

The three situations today are a request, an apology and a refusal. The six new situations are drawn
from what the scenario's own writing task and deck already imply a learner must handle: delivering
bad news, chasing something overdue, disagreeing with a decision, asking for more time, giving
credit or praise, and correcting someone's mistake. Choose six of that class; do not add a fourth
way of making a request.

Authoring rules:

- **The two halves must differ in ways a learner can name.** Contractions against none; a phrasal
  verb against its single-word twin; an apology carried by one word against one carried by a formula;
  a direct ask against a hedged one; an agentless passive that moves the person away from the error.
  A pair whose only difference is politeness teaches nothing — that is the thing the scenario's own
  rehearsal explicitly forbids as an answer.
- **Every one of the twelve carries a `tip`, and the tip is the mark.** Say who says it, to whom, and
  what it costs to get that wrong — "fine in a text and wrong in a covering letter" is the model, and
  it is already in this scenario's deck. A tip that translates the line does not count.
- **Adjacency is load-bearing.** Both renderers walk the array in order, so a pair is only legible if
  its two halves sit next to each other. Insert each new pair as two consecutive entries, casual
  first, and keep the existing slug convention that names which half an entry is — Task 2 gates it.
- **New authored slugs only**, unique within the scenario. Do not touch the six existing entries: they
  are correct, they are live keys, and their pairing already works.
- Nothing here repeats a line from the scenario's writing model answer, its rehearsal, or its deck.

**Regenerate the id fixture in this same commit.** Twelve new scheduled ids land here, and the gate
fails an id that is in the tree and not in the fixture — that is the mechanism by which this phase's
own ids stay inside the gate it created. Run
`node --experimental-strip-types scripts/verify-id-stability.mts --update` and confirm the diff is
**additions only**, with no changed hash on any of the six pre-existing entries, before committing it
beside the content.

Then raise `minutes` for `native/register` to the smallest whole minute the session-length invariant
allows at 18 warm-up phrases and the deck as it stands after this commit. The gate is `>=`, so
exactly zero slack passes and is not a reason to round up.

Before committing, run a corpus-wide exact and Jaccard scan of the new phrase texts against all 35
scenarios and record the highest pair. Watch the formal halves in particular: formal English is
where every scenario in the app sounds most alike, so the Work world is the neighbourhood to check.
