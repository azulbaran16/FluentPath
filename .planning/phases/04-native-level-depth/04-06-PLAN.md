<objective>
`native/culture` is the scenario research calls dearest in raw words if it grows receptively, for a
reason that is a design constraint rather than a difficulty: **every reference dates.** A bank built
out of the references themselves has a shelf life the other four do not, and would be stale before
the beta user finishes the world.

The scenario already knows this. Its six phrases are deliberately not references but *repairs* — what
she says when one lands and she does not catch it — and its deck is the vocabulary for talking about
references rather than the references. Its passage is the one place where a reference actually goes
past, and every reference in it is insured by the sentences around it so a reader who has never seen
the thing can still recover it.

Depth here is more of that machinery, at more speeds and in more rooms. It is also the scenario whose
rehearsal is the only one of the five that escapes the world's contrastive-repetition echo, which
makes it the model rather than the outlier.

**If execution stops right after this plan**, four of the five native scenarios are deep. Only
`native/pronunciation` still reports Phase 3's floors, truthfully, because coverage is derived.

Purpose: depth that does not date.
Output: 18 repair phrases, a 24-card deck about references, and a gate on the shelf life.
</objective>

  <action>
Take the set from six phrases to eighteen, keeping the rule the header already states and extending
the situations it covers. The six today handle asking what a reference was and excusing not knowing
it. The twelve additions widen the repertoire along three axes:

- **The room.** A repair that works at lunch with four friends is the wrong one in a meeting, on a
  call where nobody can see your face, in a group chat where the moment has already passed, or with
  someone senior who assumed you would get it. Author for those rooms.
- **The speed.** Sometimes the repair has to be instant and invisible — carry on and look it up
  later. Sometimes it can be open. Sometimes the right move is to let it go entirely and say nothing,
  and the phrase is what you say two turns later instead.
- **The other direction.** She is not only the person who misses a reference; she is also the person
  whose own reference lands on nobody, and the person who can offer one back. Phrases for landing a
  reference of your own, for rescuing one that died, and for handing the turn on afterwards.

Hard constraints:

- **No item may depend on a specific film, song, programme, advert, meme, person or year.** That is
  what dates, and it is the one property this scenario cannot afford. If an item cannot be understood
  in five years without a footnote, it does not go in.
- **Nothing here names any reference the scenario's reading passage asks the reader to recover.** That
  passage insures its references by context on purpose, and glossing one here converts a reading
  exercise into recall.
- **Every one of the twelve carries a `tip`**, and the tip is the use note: who this works with, when
  it would sound like an excuse, and what it costs to use it in the wrong room.
- Nothing repeats the briefing's own examples, the deck, or the rehearsal's moves.
- New authored slugs only; the six existing entries are untouched, in every field — the fixture's
  hash covers the whole record, so a rewritten `tip` under a live id fails as a re-point.

**A. The deck to 24 cards.** The existing eight give her the words for talking about references. The
sixteen additions extend that vocabulary into the territory a C1 learner is actually stranded in:
the kinds of thing a reference can be and the social work each does — a catchphrase, an in-joke, a
running gag, a nickname that outlived its origin, a euphemism everybody knows, a pointed
understatement, a set-up everyone can finish, a deliberately obscure aside, a shared complaint that
functions as small talk, and the tonal registers that carry them, above all irony, sarcasm and
deadpan, which research names as the Sounding Native failure with no bank of its own today.

`ScenarioVocabCard` stays `{id, term, es, example}` — no new item type, per D-01. Authoring rules:

- **The `example` insures the term by its own context**, exactly as the scenario's passage insures its
  references: a reader who does not already know the term should be able to recover it from the
  sentence. That is this scenario's signature technique and the reason it is the dearest of the five
  in words per card. Budget for longer examples here than elsewhere.
- **No card names a work, a person, a brand or a year.** Same shelf-life rule as Task 1, and it binds
  harder here because a card that illustrates "a catchphrase" is tempted to quote one.
- `es` reads as Spanish, and several of these terms have no clean Spanish equivalent — where that is
  true, the `es` should say what the thing *is* rather than pretend a one-word translation exists.
- New authored slugs only; no existing card is edited, in any field.
- Twenty-four examples in one sitting is where fatigue shows; read them consecutively before
  committing and rewrite anything sharing a frame with a neighbour.
