<objective>
Two things this phase must not do quietly.

**The first is a latent overclaim.** The world page renders a skill pill with no availability flag,
so it counts *declarations* while the scenario page reads the *registry*. That has been harmless
only because 52 of 52 pairs are written. It goes live the moment a skill is declared before its bank
entry exists, which is the normal authoring order — and it is the exact failure the derived-coverage
mechanism exists to prevent. The ledger has carried it since 03-05 and says the fix is three lines,
owed to whichever Phase 4 plan first adds a scenario with an unwritten pair. This plan pays it, and
pays it first, whatever the decision below turns out to be.

**The second is a decision, not a detail.** Research found two strong candidates for a new skill
declaration in this world and priced them; it also found that "more reading" and "more rehearsals" —
both named in D-01 — split into one cheap path and one expensive one, because three of the six banks
hold exactly one item per pair all the way down to the coverage wrapper. Adding a declaration changes
the 52, trips a hard-wired literal and re-arms the ledger entry above. CONTEXT is explicit that this
is a real decision to be flagged rather than taken silently. So it is a blocking checkpoint with the
prices attached, and the option this plan recommends deferring is on the list rather than absent
from it.

**If execution stops right after this plan**, the world page tells the truth about every pair, and
whatever the human approved is written and wired. Nothing is ever left declared-but-unwritten,
because the plan's central rule is that a declaration and its bank land in one commit.

**This plan has a declared splitting point.** It touches ten files and its checkpoint can approve up
to six options, several of them ~631 authored words each. On three or more approvals it splits into
`04-08a` (world-page fix plus the cheap options) and `04-08b` (the reading passages), before any
authoring begins — the branch is written into the checkpoint below so that the executor does not have
to decide under a filling context window whether it is still safe to continue.

Purpose: close a latent overclaim, and take the container decisions in the open.
Output: an honest world page, and the approved declarations with their content.
</objective>

Implement exactly the options approved in Task 1, in the order they were approved, **one commit per
option**. Two rules govern every one of them.

**Rule one — a declaration and its bank land in the same commit, never in two.** Adding a skill to a
scenario without its bank entry is a live overclaim on the catalogue, and it is the normal authoring
order, which is why this has to be a rule rather than an intention. In the same commit as the
declaration: the bank entry, the harness's pair-count literal raised to the new total, and the
harness group below.

**Rule two — a container change gets its own reading of the code before a line is written.** For the
writing option, the record type, the accessor's return type, the coverage wrapper and the one call
site all move together, and the wrapper's item list goes from a single-element literal to the array
itself, which is the whole distinction between a bank that can grow and one that cannot. The summary
string changes from one task to N by derivation and by nothing else, and the desk reveals its picker
at two prompts on its own.

Per-option authoring notes:

- **A reading passage** is the most expensive unit in the project and the one whose design is most
  easily lost. Keep the technique both native passages already use: the questions ask what an
  expression means *in this text*, the glossary is silent on exactly those and glosses only what
  would block the reader on the way, and every question carries an explanation written as
  substantive prose rather than to a formula. Nothing in the passage may repeat the scenario's own
  phrases, deck or briefing — and after this phase those banks are three times the size they were,
  so check them rather than remembering them.
- **A grammar set** reuses an existing topic string character for character where the teaching point
  matches, or introduces at most one new permanent string; either way the topic sets 04-02 asserted
  must be extended in the same commit, or the harness fails, which is the gate doing its job. It is
  also the only option that adds scheduled ids, so **regenerate the id fixture in the same commit**
  through `scripts/verify-id-stability.mts --update` and confirm the diff is additions only — an id
  in the tree and not in the fixture fails as an unrecorded addition, deliberately.
- **Extra writing tasks** each need a brief with a real reader, a word range at the scenario's level,
  a checklist that names what the draft must contain, and an original model answer that would itself
  pass that checklist. The existing task is the strongest prompt in the corpus; the additions have
  to stand beside it, and the checklist for a register task is made of differences rather than of
  merits.
- **A reworked rehearsal** keeps its setup, exactly three moves and a success line the speaker can
  check against her own output with nothing listening. Move it off contrastive repetition onto one
  of the other devices — a constraint or ban, a count, a time or length budget, a predict-then-check,
  or a deliberate repair caught mid-sentence — and keep the existing slug, because a speaking id is
  unscheduled and the point of the change is the wording rather than the key.

**Then append one harness group covering whatever landed**, following the two-edit rule: the new
pair is declared and written; every key in the affected bank names a pair that declares that skill;
and the new content is distinct from every other scenario's at the paragraph or move grain the
existing groups already use.

**The pair-count literal stays a literal.** There are two of them and both are tripwires, which is
their whole value:

- `DECLARED_PAIRS.length === 52` at `scripts/verify-scenario-content.mts:520`, and
- `COVERAGE_TOTALS.pairsTotal === 52` in the coverage-totals group further down.

Each one is **raised by hand to the new total in the same commit as the declaration** — 53, or 54,
whatever the approved options produce. Do NOT replace either with a comparison against the thing it
is checking. `DECLARED_PAIRS.length === DECLARED_PAIRS.length` is a tautology that can never fail, and
swapping the literal for it would permanently disarm the one check that makes a silent change to the
curriculum's declarations fail loudly. `AGENTS.md` forbids weakening an assertion; this is what
weakening it would look like. A hand-raised literal that has to be edited deliberately is the point,
not a defect to be engineered away.

The cross-check `COVERAGE_TOTALS.pairsTotal === DECLARED_PAIRS.length` **already exists** beside the
second literal and asserts a real relation — the registry agrees with the curriculum — so it is kept
as-is and is not a substitute for either literal. Keep both: the literal catches a declaration
changing at all, the cross-check catches the registry and the curriculum drifting apart.

What this plan genuinely ADDS is one assertion this phase has earned and Phase 3 could not: **that no
pair is pending.** Phase 3 kept that number reported rather than asserted because it moved on almost
every plan; this phase starts at zero pending and never leaves a pair unwritten, so asserting it is
now correct.

If context runs short, stop after a **complete** option — declaration, bank, literal and assertions
all in — and record exactly which approved options remain. Stopping between an option's declaration
and its bank is the one outcome this plan does not permit.
