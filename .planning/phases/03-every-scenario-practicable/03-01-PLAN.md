<objective>
Make one scenario — `social/small-talk` — genuinely practicable end to end, and build the four
pieces of plumbing the other thirty-four scenarios and fifty-two pairs will ride on: stable
authored ids, a namespaced SRS key space, a coverage registry that derives what is written from
what the banks hold, and a committed harness that gates all of it.

This is the tracer. It is deliberately one scenario wide and the whole stack deep: authored
content → composed id → `recordAttempt` → `state.srs` → the merge → the review flow. Everything
after this plan is expansion along a path that has already been proven.

Purpose: RESEARCH names three pieces of plumbing that CONT-01 and CONT-02 cannot be satisfied
without, and all three are cheapest to get wrong now and most expensive to get wrong after ~550
items exist. D-05 is the sharpest: today `ReviewView`, `ReviewHub` and `Dashboard` resolve every
due id through the global grammar bank, so a scenario phrase would be stored correctly, merged
correctly, scheduled correctly — and be invisible. D-03 is the other: a derived coverage registry
means execution can stop after any later plan without the app claiming practice it does not have.

Output: `social/small-talk` with its own six phrases and eight vocabulary cards feeding the review
queue; a coverage registry reporting 9/35 scenarios with phrases, 1/35 with vocabulary and 0/52
pairs written; an honest "not written yet" panel wherever a declared skill has no exercise; and
`scripts/verify-scenario-content.mts` standing behind all of it.

**What the learner gains if execution stops right after this plan:** one scenario that is fully
practicable and feeds spaced repetition, every scenario page telling the truth about what it does
and does not offer, and — deliberately — the twenty-six scenarios with no phrase set of their own
no longer being handed a generic per-world set dressed up as scenario practice. That last one is a
temporary reduction in what is shown and an increase in what is true; plans 02–04 close it.
</objective>

<task type="checkpoint:decision" gate="blocking">
  <name>Task 1: The SRS id format — a one-way door on live learner data</name>
  <decision>
    What shape do the ids of scenario phrases, vocabulary cards and exercise items take in
    `state.srs` and `state.attempts`?
  </decision>
  <context>
    The beta user has real progress in Postgres today. `state.srs` and `state.attempts` share one
    flat, unnamespaced key space whose current occupants are the thirty-nine global grammar ids.
    This phase adds roughly 490 keys in CONT-02 alone and up to ~960 by the end of CONT-01.

    Once a learner's box and due date are stored under a key, changing the key shape orphans that
    entry: the item re-enters at box 0 and the schedule she earned is gone. `mergeProgress` unions
    keys blindly, so an orphan is not detected, and the reconcile re-runs on every authenticated
    load. Undoing this later is not a refactor — it is a client-side remap that must run on every
    device and interact with the merge. That is why it is being asked before anything is written.

    RESEARCH §3 recommends option A with HIGH confidence, derived from the repo's own conventions:
    the composite scenario key already identifies a scenario in `completed`, in the phrase bank, in
    the lesson bank and in the tutor link.
  </context>
  <options>
    <option id="option-a">
      <name>Composite: scenario key, item kind, authored slug — `social/small-talk#phrase#hows-it-going` (recommended)</name>
      <pros>Cannot collide with the existing grammar ids or the deck-card ids, because none of them
      contains a slash. Carries its own provenance, so the review surface resolves an id back to a
      scenario with no second lookup table. Survives the schema's key sanitiser and the merge
      unchanged. Puts the selection metadata in the id, which is the only place it can live without
      adding a field to the `{box, due}` value — and that pair merges as a unit.</pros>
      <cons>Ids are long: about 40 characters each. At ~960 keys that is measurable but small
      against the 1 MiB body cap, whose worst measured case is 19%.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="option-b">
      <name>Opaque global slugs — `sp-smalltalk-01`</name>
      <pros>Short. Flat, like the existing grammar ids.</pros>
      <cons>Provenance is gone: the review surface needs a lookup table mapping every id back to a
      scenario, which is a second thing to keep in step with the banks. Collision safety depends on
      author discipline rather than on structure. Renumbering on insert is exactly the trap that
      index-derived ids fall into.</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="option-c">
      <name>Content-hash ids — a hash of the phrase text</name>
      <pros>No authoring burden; guaranteed unique.</pros>
      <cons>The id moves whenever the content is edited. A typo fix silently orphans the learner's
      box-4 item. This is the failure mode the phase is trying to avoid, arrived at by another
      route.</cons>
    </option>
  </options>
  <resume-signal>Select: option-a, option-b, or option-c</resume-signal>
</task>
