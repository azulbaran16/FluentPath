<objective>
Close the reading half of CONT-01 with the four C1 passages: `social/humor`, `academic/articles`,
`native/idioms` and `native/culture`.

Purpose: reading is the most expensive skill per pair in this phase, and these four are the most
expensive within it — C1 passages must be long enough to be worth reading and their questions must
turn on inference rather than lookup. They were deliberately kept together so one plan carries the
whole C1 load rather than spreading it thin.

Boundary, restated because it applies to two of these four: Sounding Native's deeper native-level
treatment is CONT-04 and belongs to Phase 4. `native/idioms` and `native/culture` are authored here
at their declared C1 level and to the same shape as every other reading pair, not to Phase 4's depth.

Output: nine of nine reading pairs written.

**What the learner gains if execution stops right after this plan:** reading is complete — every one
of the nine scenarios that declares it opens into a text chosen for it with a key that explains
itself. Only speaking pairs remain unwritten, and those scenarios already carry their own phrases and
vocabulary from CONT-02.

**This plan shares wave 8 with 03-09 and must not assert a global written total.** Both plans depend
on 03-07 and neither depends on the other; their `files_modified` are disjoint, so they may merge in
either order. Reading's four pairs and speaking's fourteen both land in this wave, which means any
"N of 52 written" assertion in either plan is really an assertion about which one merged first — and
whichever lost the race would fail a gate that has nothing wrong with its own work. Every gate here
therefore counts **reading** pending pairs, which is a statement about this plan's own subject and is
true regardless of order. For the record, the wave ends at 36 of 52 written whichever way round it
runs: eighteen from 03-07, four from here, fourteen from 03-09.
</objective>
